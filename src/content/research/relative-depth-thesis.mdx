My Bachelor's thesis, written in the [CVML group](https://www.maths.lu.se/english/research/research-areas/computer-vision-and-machine-learning/)
at Lund University under [Viktor Larsson](https://vlarsson.github.io/).

## Problem

Given two images of the same scene and a set of dense feature matches between
them, can we estimate **relative depth** at the matched points — i.e. for each
match, which of the two corresponding 3D points is closer to the camera — without
running full multi-view stereo?

## Approach

The thesis trains two complementary families of models on this signal:

- **Graph Neural Networks** over the match graph, with each match as a node
  and edges encoding geometric / appearance similarity between matches.
- **Convolutional Neural Networks** working directly on local image patches
  around each match.

Both are compared against existing relative-depth methods from the CVML
literature. Implementation in PyTorch + PyTorch Geometric, with feature
extraction and matching via OpenCV.

## Reflections

The project was my first sustained piece of computer-vision research and the
reason I'm continuing in visual computing at DTU. A lot of the work was
infrastructure — preparing match datasets, sanity-checking baselines, comparing
across published methods with PhD students in the group — but the comparisons
themselves taught me more than any paper-reading would have.
